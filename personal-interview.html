SECTION 01

Career story & transitions

Build a positive narrative around your early upskilling, your move to CGI, and your growth ambitions at Lowe's.

Q01
01 • Career story & transitions

Tell me about yourself.

Suggested Spoken Answer

Sure. I'm a backend software engineer with over eight years of experience, primarily working with Java, Spring Boot, microservices, and distributed systems.

I started my career at STIXIS and later worked with CGI, HCLTech, IBM, and currently Lowe's India.

At Lowe's, I'm working on enterprise retail platforms where I contribute to backend architecture, microservices development, code reviews, and mentoring eight engineers. One of my key achievements was reducing API p99 latency from eight seconds to around 170 milliseconds using distributed Redis caching.

I've also worked with Kafka, PostgreSQL, Kubernetes, Azure, and CI/CD technologies. Alongside backend development, I've gained production experience with AI agents and MCP integrations.

At this stage, I'm looking for an opportunity where I can contribute hands-on while taking greater technical ownership and progressing toward a stronger technical leadership role.

Q02
01 • Career story & transitions

Why did you leave STIXIS after just six months?

Suggested Spoken Answer

STIXIS was my first professional experience, where I worked on developing a customer-facing B2B portal. During that time, I was also upgrading my skills by completing a course focused on Java, Spring Boot, and microservices.

After completing that training, I explored opportunities through off-campus recruitment and successfully secured a position at CGI. CGI offered me the opportunity to work on enterprise banking applications, which aligned with my career interests.

I subsequently spent nearly three years there, so that early move was an important step in building my long-term career in Java backend development.

Q03
01 • Career story & transitions

Suggested Spoken Answer

After completing my Java, Spring Boot, and microservices training, I wanted to apply those skills in an enterprise development environment.

I cleared CGI's off-campus recruitment process and received an opportunity to work on banking applications. It was a valuable transition because I gained experience working with Java backend systems, REST APIs, enterprise business logic, and production support.

Currently, at Lowe's, my responsibilities include distributed systems, technical leadership, and production ownership. Each experience has contributed to my technical development.

At this stage, I'm looking for greater alignment between my responsibilities, career progression, and compensation, with the intention of building a longer-term career in the right organization.

Java expertise & technical leadership

Reinforce that Java backend engineering is your core specialization and support leadership claims with your real project experience.

Suggested Spoken Answer

Absolutely. My core expertise is Java backend development, and that's where I've spent most of my professional career.

I've worked with Java 8, Java 17, and Java 21, along with Spring Boot, REST APIs, Kafka, Redis, and distributed systems.

Although I've gained additional experience with AI agents and MCP, I consider those complementary skills. I genuinely enjoy backend engineering, particularly solving challenges related to system performance, scalability, reliability, and architecture.

So I'm completely comfortable with a role focused entirely on Java and microservices.

Even AI-driven enterprise applications require secure APIs, scalable microservices, databases, event-driven systems, and reliable backend infrastructure.

My primary professional strength remains Java backend engineering. I want to continue developing that expertise, regardless of whether the application includes AI functionality.

I remain hands-on with implementation while supporting the team with technical decisions, code quality, and production issues.

This combination of individual technical contribution and team mentorship has helped me develop leadership capabilities.

This was an important improvement in application responsiveness and backend performance. It also reinforced the importance of measuring performance and choosing suitable architectural optimizations.

My leadership approach focuses on remaining technically involved while helping engineers deliver maintainable, reliable solutions.

Q19
02 • Java expertise & technical leadership

How do you handle disagreements during code review?

Suggested Spoken Answer

I try to keep technical discussions objective and focused on engineering requirements.

I first understand the reasoning behind the proposed implementation. Then I evaluate aspects such as maintainability, performance, security, testing, and consistency with our architecture.

If there are multiple valid approaches, I prefer discussing the trade-offs and agreeing on a solution based on technical evidence. My goal is to maintain code quality while supporting constructive collaboration.

Q20
02 • Java expertise & technical leadership

How do you handle production incidents?

Suggested Spoken Answer

My first priority is to understand the impact and restore service stability.

I start by checking monitoring dashboards, logs, error patterns, and the affected services. I coordinate with the relevant engineers to identify the issue and determine the safest mitigation.

After service recovery, I focus on root-cause analysis and preventive improvements. At Lowe's, I've worked on production incident response and monitoring using Grafana, Kibana, and Prometheus.

02 • Java expertise & technical leadership

Suggested Spoken Answer

At Lowe's, I was involved in supporting production operations during the Black Friday and Cyber Monday event.

The retail platform processed approximately 2.3 million transactions across the entire event. My responsibilities included on-call incident response and supporting the reliability of backend services.

This gave me valuable experience working with business-critical systems where monitoring, incident coordination, and reliability are especially important.

Suggested Spoken Answer

However, my expectation is based on the responsibilities and experience I bring rather than only a percentage increase. My current role includes backend architecture, mentoring engineers, feature delivery, performance optimization, and production ownership.

I've also worked on measurable improvements in API performance and engineering quality. I'm looking for compensation that reflects my experience and the responsibilities of the new role.

That said, I'm open to a reasonable discussion based on your compensation structure.

For example, at Lowe's, I contributed to reducing API p99 latency from eight seconds to 170 milliseconds and improving automated testing coverage from 46% to 80%.

I believe these experiences position me to contribute to both technical delivery and engineering quality. I'm open to discussing the package based on the scope and expectations of the role.

SECTION 04

Competing-offer scenarios

Use this section if you genuinely have another offer. Be transparent about the status, terms, deadlines, and decisions; never bluff.

Q39
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

You already have an offer. Why are you still looking for other opportunities?

Suggested Spoken Answer

Yes, I currently have an offer, and I'm grateful for that opportunity. However, I'm not making a career decision based only on the first offer I receive or on compensation alone.

At this stage, I want the right combination of technical responsibilities, career progression, organizational stability, and compensation. My core expertise is Java, Spring Boot, microservices, and distributed systems, so meaningful technical ownership matters to me.

That's why I'm evaluating opportunities carefully before making a final decision. If your role aligns well with my goals, I would absolutely consider it seriously.

PREPARATION NOTE: Use this only if you actually hold an offer. If you don't, use the honest 'no confirmed offer' answer in Q33.
Q40
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

If your other offer is ₹40 LPA, why should we offer you more?

Suggested Spoken Answer

I appreciate that compensation is important, but I'm not trying to create a bidding competition. I evaluate roles based on engineering challenges, responsibilities, growth opportunities, and the overall compensation structure.

If the role is a strong match, I'd be happy to discuss a competitive package that is appropriate for its scope and expectations.

PREPARATION NOTE: The ₹40 LPA figure is a hypothetical interviewer question, not a claim about your actual offer. Never invent or inflate a competing offer.
Q41
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

If we match your existing offer, would you choose us?

Suggested Spoken Answer

Yes, I would definitely consider your opportunity. If compensation is comparable, my decision would depend on the technical ownership, the systems I'd be working on, the team's engineering culture, and the long-term growth path.

I'd want to make a well-informed commitment rather than choose based only on the number in the offer letter.

Q42
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

Are you using our offer to negotiate with another company?

Suggested Spoken Answer

I'm evaluating opportunities seriously because I want to make the right long-term move. I'm interested in the role itself, not just in collecting offers.

My decision would be based on the complete opportunity - the technical scope, team, career progression, and compensation - and I'd communicate my decision professionally and within the agreed timeline.

PREPARATION NOTE: Do not categorically deny negotiating if you are in fact using offers for that purpose. Be truthful about your process.
Q43
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

Choose depending on your real situation:
Spoken Response (IF NOT ACCEPTED)

I haven't accepted it yet. I'm reviewing the opportunity and its terms before making a final commitment.

Spoken Response (IF ACCEPTED)

Yes, I've accepted an offer, but I'm still reassessing whether it's the right long-term fit. I recognize the commitment involved, so I'm handling this carefully and would make any decision transparently and professionally.

PREPARATION NOTE: Use only the version matching the real status. Never claim you have not accepted if you already have.

04 • Competing-offer scenarios

Q45
04 • Competing-offer scenarios

Can you share your offer letter, and how long do you have to decide?

Suggested Spoken Answer

I understand why you're asking. I can discuss the genuine offer terms and the date by which I need to respond. My current decision deadline is [actual date].

I'd prefer not to circulate another employer's confidential offer letter during an early discussion, but I'm happy to understand what verification your process requires at a later stage. Could you also share the expected timeline for your interview and offer process?

PREPARATION NOTE: Do not share confidential or personal documents unless you are comfortable and permitted to do so. Give a real deadline, not one invented to pressure a recruiter.
